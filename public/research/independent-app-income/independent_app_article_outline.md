# Article outline: The Income Distribution of Independent Apps

**Search title:** How Many Indie App Developers Make $1,000 a Month?  
**Display title:** The Income Distribution of Independent Apps  
**Deck:** A denominator-by-denominator estimate of app and developer revenue, with a reproducible model and public case ledger.

## Opening

A defensible provisional answer is **about one in six serious independent developers**. The plausible range is roughly **one in twelve to one in four**. Count every store listing instead and the answer falls toward **one in sixty publishers** and **one in one hundred apps**. The difference is not rhetoric. It is the denominator.

The stores do not publish a table connecting each live app to its owner, revenue model, current monthly receipts, costs, and portfolio siblings. Every clean-looking percentage on this topic therefore hides a population choice. This investigation makes those choices visible.

## Proposed structure

1. **The answer, with the denominator attached**  
   Headline app- and developer-level ranges; current versus sustained revenue.

2. **Six different markets hiding inside “the app market”**  
   A-F denominator ladder: all listed, active, active monetized, independent active, serious attempts, and 12-month maintained.

3. **What the sources actually observe**  
   Official store counts, CMA billing evidence, RevenueCat, Adapty, intelligence vendors, verified dashboards, surveys, postmortems, and academic work.

4. **The modeled distribution**  
   Revenue bands from $0 to $100,000+; median versus mean; why no single “average app revenue” is defensible.

5. **Apps are not developers**  
   Multi-app portfolios, correlated outcomes, threshold crossing by aggregation, and dependence on one hit.

6. **The anatomy of $1,000 a month**  
   Subscribers, installs, MAU, GMV, paid downloads, and gross-to-profit bridges.

7. **Observed apps near, above, and below the line**  
   Current verified cases, low-revenue portfolios, zero-revenue apps, sale/disclosure bias.

8. **Where the odds differ**  
   Games/non-games, subscription/ads/IAP/paid, iOS/Android, mature/new, organic/paid, pre-existing audience/cold start.

9. **Why the public record looks richer than the market is**  
   Survivorship, marketplace selection, vanished failures, screenshots without costs, and repeated attempts by the same developer.

10. **What is known, modeled, and unresolved**  
    Claim ledger, sensitivity, and the missing representative owner-app-revenue panel.

## Publication labels

Use visible labels in the article:

- **Observed fact**: directly reported by a source within its stated population.
- **Derived calculation**: arithmetic from observed inputs.
- **Modeled estimate**: depends on population-transfer or persistence assumptions.
- **Interpretation**: reasoned reading of the evidence.
- **Speculation**: plausible but not source-constrained; minimize and mark clearly.

## Recommended lead charts

1. Interval plot: share over $1,000 by denominator, apps versus developers.
2. Survival curve: any revenue, $100, $1,000, $5,000, $10,000, $25,000, $100,000.
3. Stacked bars: modeled revenue bands across denominators.
4. Bridge: ever hit $1,000 -> current month -> three-month sustained.
5. Subscriber requirements at $4.99, $9.99, and $19.99.
6. Ad model: MAU needed under conservative, base, and strong engagement/eCPM.
7. Case scatter: current revenue against app age or active subscriptions.
8. Gross-to-proceeds-to-profit waterfall.
9. Selection-bias funnel for public founder evidence.
10. Portfolio dependency: share of successful developers dominated by one app.

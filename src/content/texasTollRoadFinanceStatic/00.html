Build the road, collect tolls, repay the road, remove the toll. That is the model many drivers carry in their heads. It assumes one project, one construction bill, one loan, and a clean ending. The major Texas systems examined here pool roads, refinance debt, hold reserves, fund later capital work, transfer legally available cash, or operate under concession contracts whose terms last for decades.

SH 288 shows the break most clearly. Texas already held public title to the corridor, yet paid $1,731,730,721, less permitted adjustments, to terminate the private concession in 2024. Control transitioned to the public structure and tolling continued. The transaction changed contractual revenue rights and financing; it did not erase the state's legal power to charge.

“Paid off” is five different questions

A construction-cost calculation, a road's debt balance, a system's debt, a public body's tolling power, and a concessionaire's revenue rights are not interchangeable. A road can satisfy one test and remain tolled because another layer is still active.

The first test is historical economics. The other four determine whether the current revenue stream remains pledged, legally authorized, or contractually assigned.

# What Happened to the Software Buyout Boom?

This research package audits 25 large sponsor-led software take-private transactions announced from 2020 through 2022. Company-reported headline transaction values total $171.2 billion.

At the August 17, 2026 evidence cutoff, no transaction meets the study's control-based definition of a clean sponsor realization. That is an exit-inventory finding, not a return finding. The public record supports classifications of current control and documented ownership events; it does not support a cohort MOIC, IRR, or impairment estimate.

## Classification rules

- Headline transaction value sizes the cohort. It is not sponsor equity invested, debt funded, current enterprise value, net asset value, or realized proceeds.
- A completed sale or distribution that removes the original sponsor group's control can qualify as a clean realization.
- An IPO with continuing sponsor voting control is a liquidity route, not a clean realization.
- Partial stake sales, dividend recapitalizations, refinancings, and continuation vehicles are ownership or liquidity events. They do not by themselves prove a gain or loss.
- Creditor ownership is classified separately from sponsor realization.

## Headline result

The cohort produced liquidity events and ownership changes, but conventional control exits remain scarce. The strongest defensible conclusion is therefore narrower than a claim that the vintage succeeded or failed: the original control inventory largely remains unresolved, and public evidence is insufficient to convert that fact into a return estimate.

## Evidence boundary

The $171.2 billion total combines mixed company-reported transaction-value definitions. It should not be used as a denominator for equity returns. Sponsor cash flows, debt paydown, add-on investment, dividend proceeds, continuation-vehicle pricing, fund marks, and carried-interest allocations are generally not public at the level required for a cohort return calculation.

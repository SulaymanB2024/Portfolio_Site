Three Investment Pitches: Operating Mechanisms and Return Hurdles

tl;dr

Toast: the trailing-year model yields $37/share in the base and a $24.33 entry hurdle for a 15% annual return. Harvey: the $15.5bn equivalent entry needs $51.43bn exit equity for a 20% five-year return after 25% dilution. Vanta: the base cohort model gives 17.4% at a $4.15bn equivalent entry, or a $3.71bn maximum entry for the 20% hurdle. These are conditions, not probability-weighted forecasts.

Context & Methods

Company data was verified October 12, 2026. Primary financial and product sources are registered alongside each manuscript. The Toast quote is a separately dated secondary market readout.

Key Assumptions

inputs.json separates reported anchors, derived quantities, missing data and future assumptions. $400m Harvey starting ARR is only an illustrative normalization. Vanta combines an April 2026 ARR checkpoint with an older financing benchmark for hypothetical underwriting; it does not reconstruct the Series D investor's return. Private models exclude security preferences, fees and secondary premiums.

Takeaways

A useful product and a good investment require separate evidence. Toast's cash acquisition and physical deployment matter. Harvey's value must reach customer contribution and durable margins. Vanta's recurring job must survive the first audit. Real private-company underwriting still needs verified financials, cash requirements and security terms.

## Crocs: cash generation, not a rescue by HEYDUDE

Crocs at **$116.90** is the strongest long in this group because the operating franchise already generates substantial cash. It does not require a dramatic HEYDUDE turnaround or a higher multiple to work. The investment case must nonetheless distinguish revenue recovery, profit recovery and the boost to per-share results from a smaller share count.

The FY2025 annual filing plus matched H1 columns gives June 2026 TTM revenue of **$4,054.866 million**. Crocs brand accounts for **$3,372.461 million** and HEYDUDE **$682.405 million**. Within Crocs, North America contributes **$1,688.647 million**, international **$1,683.814 million**, DTC **$1,827.250 million** and wholesale **$1,545.211 million**. These are derived period aggregates, not a forecast or annualization of the strongest quarter. The supporting rows are [the annual filing’s revenue note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1334036/000133403626000006/crox-20251231.htm) and [the June 2026 filing’s matched interim note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1334036/000133403626000052/crox-20260630.htm).

TTM cash from operations is **$762.634 million**. Subtract **$58.014 million** of cash capex and **$41.517 million** of SBC added back in CFO to obtain the author’s **$663.103 million owner-cash proxy**. On the last disclosed July 23 share count of **47.945075 million**, the frozen price implies approximately **$5,604.779 million** in equity value and an **11.83%** proxy yield. Balance-sheet cash is a June number: **$170.276 million unrestricted**, with **$3.555 million restricted** excluded. Debt is **$1,334.000 million principal** versus **$1,307.658 million carrying value**. The proxy is after interest and all capex; do not subtract debt again from the equity value used for a levered cash yield. Debt principal repayments still compete with future buybacks.

The normalization should be cautious. FY2024 owner cash was **$890.086 million**, FY2025 **$622.499 million**, and current TTM **$663.103 million**. H1 CFO improved **$52.203 million**, while the income-tax working-capital line improved **$81.055 million**. A **$600 million recurring annual cash anchor** is a defensible author assumption below reported TTM, not a company target. Do not attribute the recent cash improvement wholly to stronger consumer economics.

The positive evidence is specific: Crocs’ international DTC business is expanding, North America stopped declining in Q2, and HEYDUDE’s DTC growth is offsetting some wholesale weakness. But H1 unit-volume commentary remains negative, DTC adds store/rent costs, and consolidated Q2 adjusted operating profit fell. Q2 adjusted net income fell **5.0%** while adjusted EPS rose **7.6%** as diluted shares fell **11.6%**. Crocs’ adjusted EPS retains SBC; the cash proxy deducts it because CFO adds it back. [The Q2 release and reconciliations](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1334036/000133403626000050/croxq22026-pressrelease.htm) support those distinctions.

The latest FY2026 guidance gives **$12.47–12.77 GAAP EPS** versus **$13.70–14.00 adjusted EPS**; the tax rates are **23% GAAP** and **18% adjusted**, and further buybacks are excluded. At the midpoint, the frozen price is approximately **9.26× GAAP** and **8.44× adjusted** guidance. The release explicitly excludes potential IEEPA refunds. Approximately **$20 million was received after June 30**, with recognition expected in Q3 cost of sales. It is absent from June cash/TTM. Do not subtract that refund again from already refund-excluding guidance; exclude it when normalizing subsequent reported cash or profits.

The overlooked downside is the tax balance. June has **$639.580 million long-term income taxes payable**; FY2025 has **$636.973 million unrecognized tax benefits**, substantially connected with intellectual-property tax positions. These are distinct from face debt, operating leases and deferred tax assets. Their settlement timing and cash amount are uncertain. A **$600 million additional cash-settlement stress** within two years is a useful severe scenario, not a prediction of what a tax authority will collect and not an additional reported liability on top of the balance already disclosed.

The root’s model should allocate recurring cash to debt reduction and a tax reserve before funding net share-count reduction. Our illustrative unchanged-multiple sensitivity uses $600 million normalized current owner cash, $650 million in 2028 and 40.5 million terminal shares: approximately **$149.92**, or **28.25%** price return over two years. Holding cash at $600 million gives materially less return; growth matters. An extra $600 million cash use at a hypothetical $132 average avoided net buyback price raises terminal shares to about 45.05 million and reduces that sensitivity to **$134.79**. The root’s cash-budget model supersedes these simple examples. Crocs buys treasury stock rather than legally cancelling it, so write “reduces outstanding shares” instead of implying permanent legal retirement.

Useful disconfirmation conditions: persistent Crocs North America decline combined with decelerating international DTC; consolidated adjusted operating margin below 20%; recurring cash after SBC below $500 million; leverage rising while repurchases continue; or tax payments crowding out share reduction. These thresholds are author judgments. They should be checked against complete comparable periods and stripped of tariff refunds.

## Qualcomm: the royalty renewal is real, the AI cash is prospective

Qualcomm has the most interesting second thesis, but I would keep it outside the four selected longs until its cash conversion and the dilution budget are underwritten. At **$174.43**, it is a recovery and diversification bet rather than a simple low-P/E value stock.

June TTM CFO is **$12.401 billion**, capex **$1.985 billion**, and SBC **$3.242 billion**, leaving **$7.174 billion** in owner-cash proxy. Inventory consumed **$1.798 billion** in the first nine months while capex increased. A $5.7 billion deferred-tax valuation allowance booked in FY2025 was reversed in Q2 FY2026; annual or nine-month GAAP EPS alone is therefore misleading. Q3 also has investment gains, while adjusted EPS excludes $0.68 per share of SBC. [The annual](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/000080432825000085/qcom-20250928.htm), [June interim](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/000080432826000086/qcom-20260628.htm) and [earnings bridge](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/000080432826000085/qcom062826erex991.htm) establish the cash and accounting basis.

The September 24 patent-license renewal with Apple, effective April 1, 2027, reduces a specific QTL risk. Its term and economics remain undisclosed. The [company announcement](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/2026/09/qualcomm-announces-renewal-of-global-patent-license-agreement-wi) does not reverse the QCT modem loss. Q3 automotive and IoT growth together replace about 61% of the handset revenue lost in that quarter; QCT margins also fell. Management’s FY2029 targets of $40 billion non-handset revenue and greater than $18 adjusted EPS are beyond the two-year horizon. [The official deck](https://s204.q4cdn.com/645488518/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/FY2026-3rd-Quarter-Earnings-Presentation_7-29-26_Final.pdf) also supplies a greater-than-$14.50 GAAP counterpart, primarily reflecting SBC exclusions.

The share count is easy to mishandle. The cover reports 1,050 million common shares at July 27. Modular closed July 28 with primarily **18 million newly issued shares**, including executive shares with service conditions; Alphawave’s exchangeable shares were already presented in June financial statements. A limited 1,068 million post-Modular count is not an exact October economic dilution count. [The Amazon warrant filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/804328/000110465926105718/tm2623289d1_8k.htm) adds conditional rights to up to 25 million shares, cashless exercise allowed. The $60 billion payment level is a vesting ceiling through 2036, not guaranteed backlog or booked revenue.

Reject the current price for this batch if the investment argument needs the entire FY2029 target, an unchanged adjusted/GAAP earnings gap, or repurchases unadjusted for acquisitions and warrants. A credible later long would require segment economics, a cash-return budget after SBC and dividends, and a measured timetable for actual custom-silicon deliveries. Our $231.81 base sensitivity presumes $11 billion owner cash and 1,040 million shares in 2028, against an author-normalized $8.5 billion today; those assumptions are not accepted forecasts.

## HCA: stronger hospital demand can produce weaker shareholder cash

HCA at **$458.60** is excluded. The recurring franchise is strong, but a roughly **4.89%** TTM shareholder cash proxy yield does not offer a sufficient cushion for the visible reimbursement transition. TTM CFO of **$11.124 billion**, less capex **$5.127 billion**, SBC **$375 million** and **$767 million minority-owner distributions**, leaves **$4.855 billion**. CFO includes earnings that do not belong wholly to the public common shareholder. The [FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/860730/000119312526044769/hca-20251231.htm) and [June 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/860730/000119312526321077/hca-20260630.htm) cash statements support the calculation.

Q2 Florida recognition includes $980 million of revenue and $557 million of expense for periods before 2026. Removing that historical-period recognition mechanically leaves a much smaller current revenue increase; it is not a complete organic-growth metric. The [latest release](https://investor.hcahealthcare.com/news/news-details/2026/HCA-Healthcare-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) estimates $400 million Q2 payer-mix pretax harm while incremental Medicaid benefits provide a similar offset. Surgeries declined and same-facility uninsured admissions grew 23.4%. Lower salary/revenue ratios partly reflect recognized revenue, so salaries per employee and per admission are more useful checks.

FY2026 EPS guidance was reduced to $28.70–30.50. Buybacks support per-share earnings, but FY2025 $10.067 billion in repurchases exceeded cash after capex, SBC and minority distributions; debt carries part of the return. [CMS’s February 2 preliminary letter](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/managed-care/downloads/sdp-letter-02022026.pdf) describes state-directed-payment limits and grandfathering phase-down from rating periods beginning 2028. It does not quantify the company-wide impact. Payer attrition and this policy boundary overlap the forecast horizon. Our unchanged-cash-multiple sensitivity of $516.70 plus two years of assumed dividends produces about 6.78% annual total return, below the chosen 12% research hurdle. Patient volumes alone are insufficient to justify a long.

## Wesco: growth cannot be distributed before it is financed

Wesco at **$361.34** is excluded despite genuine data-center momentum. The latest July deck raises sales and adjusted EPS guidance while **reducing company FCF guidance from $500–800 million to $300–600 million**. Adjusted FY2026 EPS midpoint is $16.75, placing the stock at approximately 21.57×; SBC is included in that EPS and should not be deducted a second time. [The filed deck](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/929008/000092900826000021/wcc-2q2026slides.htm) is the controlling current guidance.

The [annual](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/929008/000092900826000008/wcc-20251231.htm) and [June interim](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/929008/000092900826000024/wcc-20260630.htm) give TTM CFO $264.3 million, cash capex $109.2 million and SBC $57.1 million: a $98.0 million owner proxy, **0.56%** of equity. Before working-capital changes, the corresponding proxy is $1,008.8 million, or 5.73%. That contrast identifies the underwriting problem; it does not justify treating all growth funding as expendable. Receivables, inventory and supplier prepayments require capital, while supplier payables and deferred customer revenue finance part of it.

Company H1 FCF of $245.7 million adds back $22.2 million of actual digital/restructuring cash spending; plain CFO minus capex is $223.5 million. The [release’s reconciliation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/929008/000092900826000021/wcc-2q2026earnings.htm) prevents a misleading comparison with other companies’ unadjusted FCF. Net outstanding shares rose slightly despite repurchases. Backlog growth is compelling, but absolute guaranteed purchases, cancellation terms and capital requirements were not established from these materials. An unchanged earnings-multiple sensitivity at $20 EPS in 2028 produces about 9.78% annual total return including assumed dividends, and it still fails to solve cash funding. Reconsider after collections, supplier terms and cash returns improve or the entry price compensates for them.

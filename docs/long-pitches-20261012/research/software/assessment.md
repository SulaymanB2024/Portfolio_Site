# Software and distribution candidates — research cutoff October 12, 2026

**Recommendation:** Adobe is the strongest of these four, followed by PayPal. Expedia supports a credible third long at $268.47 if its improving consumer economics and B2B growth persist. Dropbox does not offer enough margin of safety at $34.87 to displace them. This ranks candidates for the root's selection; it does not authorize trades or publication.

The entry prices are the root's frozen October 12 SIP closes. All company results predate that cutoff. Latest disclosed shares are dated separately; multiplying them by the October close is an approximate capitalization, not an observed market cap on the price date. No analyst consensus or market expectation has been invented.

## Adobe: the price needs paid-workflow durability more than an AI windfall

At this entry, I would build the long around recurring paid creative and document workflows. A generous forecast for stand-alone AI products is unnecessary. Adobe's FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance midpoint is $18.145, making the entry 13.12 times that figure. The richer non-GAAP EPS includes a different compensation adjustment and should not be used to make comparisons with PayPal look cleaner. [Q3 release](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/au56y4ter.pdf)

The cash bridge is substantial: TTM CFO $10.806bn less capex $214m produces $10.592bn FCF; subtract $2.071bn CFO-added SBC for an $8.521bn economic-cost sensitivity. TTM GAAP net income is $7.284bn and operating income $9.271bn. This cash anchor is useful because it charges compensation rather than assuming dilution costs nothing. Latest cover shares are 389.2m on September 18; balance shares fell from 413m to 391m over the first nine months, a 5.33% net reduction, against $6.821bn buyback cash. Gross shares repurchased cannot be added as another return on top of that reduction. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000003/adbe-20251128.htm), [Q3FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)

The forward question is whether useful creation becomes abundant while the workflow that finishes, governs and distributes it remains paid. The company has evidence of continuing demand, but not a complete answer to that question. Q3 customer-group subscription revenue is $4.651bn Creative & Marketing Professionals and $1.905bn Business Professionals & Consumers, up 13% and 16% respectively. ARR of $27.50bn grew 11.2%, including Semrush. AI-first ARR exceeds $650m; $650m divided by $27.50bn is a **2.36% floor comparison**, not an exact AI share or proof the share is below 2.4%. The billion MAUs also include freemium/productivity usage. Do not infer a billion paying creators. [Q3 datasheet](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/b75st5a4rwea.pdf), [prepared remarks](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/01906202/c6yetrerew.pdf)

Original underwriting insight: the long can be tested on **how little paid growth is needed**, rather than on the largest AI growth statistic. Under the explicitly artificial perpetuity diagnostic in `evidence.json`, the $8.521bn cash sensitivity needs only roughly 0.7% perpetual growth to support the price at a 10% equity discount rate. That is not a target or a claim about investor consensus; the infinite-life assumption does most of the work. A finite two-year model must still allow structural deterioration and a lower terminal multiple. The useful question is whether a company currently growing revenue and ARR in double digits suffers the sustained erosion required to make this cash base disappear.

Candidate operating cases, for the root to translate through a constrained cash/repurchase model:

- **Base:** approximately 8% annual sales growth; GAAP operating margin around 34%; cash after SBC grows 6–8%; net share reduction around 3% annually. A 2028 annual run rate around $30bn revenue and $10bn cash after compensation, with a 13-times cash multiple, suggests roughly $350 per share. GAAP EPS around $21–22 at 16 times provides a cross-check. These are author assumptions.
- **Bull:** 10–12% sales growth, paid AI attach without material price pressure, stable mid-30s GAAP margin and faster affordable net retirement. Around $480 becomes possible with a 17-times cash multiple. Do not extrapolate AI-first ARR's 150% growth across all revenue.
- **Bear:** roughly flat or declining sales, GAAP margin around 29%, cash after compensation near $6.5bn and a 10–11-times cash multiple. Around $180 is plausible despite large buybacks. Strong current cash generation does not guarantee durable pricing power.

The most useful measurable forecast is **ARR growth remaining at least 7% on the company's comparable currency basis in both FY2027 and Q3FY2028**, with GAAP operating margin above 32% and TTM cash after SBC above $9bn by the latest reported quarter before the October 2028 price evaluation. Those boundaries are specific author tests, not company guidance.

Catalysts: recurring quarterly evidence of paid growth and cash conversion; clearer AI monetization disclosures; the December 1 CEO transition. Anil Chakravarthy is scheduled to succeed Shantanu Narayen then, rather than already being CEO at the cutoff. [Transition announcement](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-announces-anil-chakravarthy-to-become-president-and-ceo)

Strongest contrary evidence: professional seats or prices could shrink even while low-priced freemium users grow; acquisition contributions can disguise organic slowing; inference costs can reduce profitability. RPO growth of about 8% is slower than subscription sales growth. Repeated ARR growth below 5%, margins below 30%, or cash after SBC below $7.5bn would undermine this underwriting. M&A must be paid for: Semrush cost $1.87bn and Topaz was pending at roughly $340m in the latest filing. They are not free AI optionality. Historical comparisons also need the FY2024 $1bn Figma termination fee; its absence helps explain FY2025 cash-flow and profit growth.

## PayPal: the cheap multiple is useful only if transaction economics recover

I would pitch PayPal as a modest operating recovery amplified by disciplined net retirement, not as a prediction that every new wallet or AI product succeeds. The entry is 10.42 times FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of about $5.38; unlike Adobe, PayPal's current non-GAAP earnings include SBC. Its Q2 underlying earnings nevertheless declined. A cheap multiple and a good brand do not establish recovery. [Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000080/pypl2q-26earningsrelease.htm), [Q2 presentation](https://s205.q4cdn.com/875401827/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/PYPL-2Q-26-Earnings-Presentation.pdf)

The stricter cash bridge is essential. TTM reported FCF $6.586bn minus $1.001bn CFO-added SBC, $128m current capitalized SBC and $653m net retained-loan cash investment leaves $4.804bn. Retained principal remains an asset; the last subtraction measures cash available for distribution, not a credit loss. BNPL-adjusted TTM FCF is $7.926bn, but company FY2026 guidance is only $6bn-plus. Do not declare the $7.9bn a recurring earning power. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000024/pypl-20251231.htm), [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000082/pypl-20260630.htm), [FY2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000021/pypl4q-25earningsrelease.htm), [Q1 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000065/pypl1q-26earningsrelease.htm)

Corporate liquidity excluding strategic investments is $13.530bn against debt $13.400bn. Customer assets and their $13.667bn cash-flow cash component belong in a separate block. Much of the remaining corporate liquidity also supports regulation and operations. The $2bn difference between customer liabilities and designated customer assets is not evidence of a missing-assets hole: the filing separately discloses $2bn Luxembourg customer balances designated for credit funding, increased by $500m in July. It also says exceptional circumstances could call on corporate liquidity. Assigning no excess-cash bonus is sensible.

Original underwriting insight: **transaction-margin dollars excluding customer interest and the cost budget are the recovery test**. Q2 total TPV rose 9% currency-neutral, but branded online checkout only 2%; TM dollars ex-customer interest rose about 3%. Venmo's 14% TPV growth is promising, but TPV is not revenue, and overlapping Venmo/branded-experience categories cannot be summed. Most tellingly, Q2 non-GAAP non-transaction opex grew 9%, faster than TM dollars. The apparent platform growth did not translate into operating leverage.

The FY2026 guidance makes this tension quantifiable. TM dollars around $15.6bn less a 7.5% midpoint increase in FY2025 non-transaction opex implies operating income near $5.83bn, versus $6.378bn in FY2025. A $1.5bn gross saving target over two to three years cannot simply be added to profit: reinvestment is explicit. EPS resilience partly reflects fewer shares. [IR newsletter](https://s205.q4cdn.com/875401827/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/PYPL-2Q-26-Earnings-IR-Newsletter.pdf)

Candidate operating cases:

- **Base:** ex-interest TM dollars grow about 5% annually from the FY2026 $14.5bn guide; customer interest stays near $1bn; non-transaction costs grow only 2%, with some savings funding product investment. GAAP earnings around $5.1bn and 760m affordable diluted shares could support EPS around $6.7; 12 times suggests approximately $81. A cash-after-reinvestment cross-check suggests roughly $84. The root must constrain share reduction to cash actually available.
- **Bull:** sustained branded recovery and Venmo monetization permit 8% ex-interest TM growth while net costs stay nearly flat; cash after compensation and loan funding exceeds $5.7bn. A 14-times cash multiple with a smaller share count suggests around $112.
- **Bear:** ex-interest TM dollars decline 3% annually, opex still rises 4%, customer interest falls and restructuring repeats. Around $35 is plausible with lower cash and an 8-times cash multiple. Capital return cannot repair deteriorating payment economics indefinitely.

A useful prospective test is **TTM TM dollars ex-customer interest reaching $16bn by Q2 2028**, with branded-checkout TPV growing at least 4% currency-neutral for two consecutive quarters and monthly active accounts returning to growth above 2%. These are author targets. A quarterly cash dividend of $0.14 is currently declared; a constant future rate would add $1.12 over eight future quarters, but future declarations remain assumptions and should be separated from the price-return benchmark.

Catalysts: quarterly evidence that checkout stabilization becomes growth; disclosed cost savings translating into slower net expense growth; continued cash-funded net retirement. Invalidation: two consecutive quarters of negative branded-checkout growth, ex-interest TM dollars below $14bn on a trailing basis, or annual strict distribution cash below $4bn while debt-funded capital return continues. New debt rose during H1; buybacks do not deserve automatic credit as value creation.

## Expedia: price the improving marketplace, not the spring deposit balance

Expedia is a reasonable long at $268.47, though less forgiving than Adobe or PayPal if margins disappoint. Its TTM $15.700bn revenue and $3.958bn adjusted EBITDA show meaningful operating scale. The latest guide raises FY2026 sales to $16.05–16.22bn with 150–175bp EBITDA-margin expansion. [Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1324424/000132442426000051/earningsrelease-q22026.htm)

The cash headline is not the cash underwriting. TTM CFO $5.216bn minus capex $757m gives $4.459bn FCF. Net travel working-capital cash is $1.399bn: deferred bookings $1.958bn + merchant payables $319m − accounts-receivable use $878m. Remove that source and $412m CFO-added SBC for $2.648bn; an alternative equity-credit SBC charge of $502m gives $2.558bn. Total AR is an imperfect travel-cycle proxy; these are sensitivities, not an exact company-normalized FCF measure. **H1 equity-book SBC is $262m for 2026 versus $248m for 2025**, verified against the equity-table period headers. Annual expensed SBC of $398m and capitalized SBC $99m do not exactly equal the parent-equity credit $488m; the scope difference remains unproven. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1324424/000132442426000008/expe-20251231.htm), [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1324424/000132442426000053/expe-20260630.htm)

Do not subtract only deferred-booking growth while ignoring receivables: that penalizes one side of the cycle. Equally, do not treat $7.127bn cash/investments as excess liquidity without recognizing $15.426bn deferred bookings and supplier commitments. Grant no excess-cash bonus. H1 cash must not be annualized, and Q2's $7.16 GAAP EPS contains investment/tax effects. The $71m Canadian DST liability reversal benefited H1 operating income; charging it again against cash without a cash reconciliation would double-count a potentially noncash item.

Original underwriting insight: the cash improvement is not solely a bet on B2B growth. **B2C currently supplies the stronger incremental profit conversion.** Q2 B2C sales rose 8% and EBITDA 22%, raising segment EBITDA margin from 29.4% to 33.2%. B2B sales rose 23% but EBITDA only 12%, reducing margin from 27.4% to 24.7%, as partner commissions and growth investment increased. Applying the consumer margin to B2B sales exaggerates the thesis. The attractive combination is a consumer platform learning to acquire demand more efficiently and a partner distribution business compounding volume on that infrastructure.

Candidate operating cases:

- **Base:** consumer sales grow 5–7%; B2B grows 13–16%; normalized cash after compensation and travel-cycle sensitivity grows around 10–12%, to roughly $3.2bn by the latest available 2028 trailing year. Net retirement is constrained to cash after dividends. Roughly 112m shares and a 12.3-times cash multiple suggests around $350. A 2028 annual operating run rate around $19bn revenue is consistent with that moderate mix case.
- **Bull:** B2B growth remains near 20% while its margin recovers toward 26–27%, consumer marketing efficiency persists and consolidated cash margins improve. About $4bn normalized cash with affordable retirement and a 14-times multiple can support roughly $480.
- **Bear:** a travel downturn combines with higher demand-acquisition costs; B2B volume grows without margin; normalized cash falls toward $2bn and net retirement slows. A 10–11-times cash multiple can put the stock near $180–190.

The measurable forecast should be **B2B trailing revenue above $6.5bn by Q2 2028 with segment EBITDA margin at least 25%, B2C margin at least 31%, and stricter normalized cash above $3bn**. Current TTM B2B sales are $5.362bn and EBITDA $1.348bn, a 25.14% margin; B2C sales $9.834bn and EBITDA $3.168bn, 32.21%. Those are useful comparable baselines. The root can raise or lower the author targets but should keep segment economics distinct.

Catalysts: quarterly B2C marketing leverage and B2B margin stabilization; peak-season earnings confirming annual cash conversion; recurring authorized repurchases that produce actual net retirement. The strongest downside evidence would be growing bookings with flat cash earnings, sustained B2B margins below 23%, or rising marketing costs that reverse B2C leverage. A consumer travel slowdown can overwhelm operating leverage. Current cash dividends of $0.48 per quarter imply $3.84 over eight future quarters only if future declarations stay constant.

## Dropbox: the adjustment produces less equity cash than the headline suggests

Dropbox's $34.87 price needs too much proof of a new growth engine relative to the alternatives. Q2 revenue excluding FormSwift grew 1.7% reported but only 0.1% constant currency; comparable ARR growth was 0.2%. Paying users grew sequentially for a third quarter, but this is stabilization, not evidence of rapid paid AI monetization. The latest annual revenue guide remains roughly flat. [Q2 release](https://investors.dropbox.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dropbox-announces-second-quarter-2026-results), [August guidance](https://investors.dropbox.com/static-files/ed86c070-4173-4339-ad09-aca866a36774), [prepared remarks](https://investors.dropbox.com/static-files/47c520a5-a888-4a35-98d2-d20440d56518)

TTM levered FCF is $957.1m. Subtract $139m equipment-finance-lease principal and $304.4m SBC for a $513.7m cash sensitivity, only 6.53% at the frozen capitalization. The Q2 headline unlevered FCF of $283.5m adds $48.3m tax-adjusted debt interest to levered FCF $235.2m; H1 adds $81.4m. Interest and equipment financing are not optional equity cash costs. June term-loan principal is $2.6748bn and 2028 note principal is **$693.3m**, rather than the $690.7m net carrying value. Debt principal less cash/investments is about $2.254bn, excluding finance leases. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467623/000146762326000008/dbx-20251231.htm), [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467623/000146762326000049/dbx-20260630.htm)

The aggressive net retirement is real, but leveraged recapitalization does not create new operating earnings. June balance shares exclude the unvested co-founder award, while the August cover's 225.709m shares includes it; avoid comparing those bases as ordinary dilution. Convertible hedges and warrants also require care if valuing a bull case above their thresholds.

I would reconsider this candidate with constant-currency core ARR growth above 3% for several quarters, explicit paid Dash monetization, cash after equipment leases/SBC above $650m and a lower entry or reduced leverage. A base flat-sales case with $520–560m cash, slower retirement and a 12-times multiple supports roughly $28–32, below the entry. A successful AI case could reach $45–50 with materially higher cash and a 14-times multiple, while renewed decline with $350–400m cash and a 10-times multiple could lead toward $18–22. Those are candidate scenarios, not a selected forecast. The burden of proof is materially higher than the cash yield suggests.

## Handoff and verification

- `inputs.json` contains read primary facts with explicit units, fiscal periods, source IDs and text anchors.
- `evidence.json` merges those facts, primary-source URLs/report dates/snapshot hashes and reproduced TTM/valuation bridges.
- `build_evidence.py` executes the bridges with Decimal arithmetic and verifies the material PayPal, Expedia, Adobe and Dropbox cash identities. It completed successfully.
- `acquisition.json` records source hashes and successful reads. `sources/` retains HTML/PDF snapshots and extracted text; large raw PDFs are research evidence, not needed in the public download or site build.
- All forecast cases and thresholds above are **author candidates**. The root owns final selection, cash-budget constraints, probabilities if any, entry/horizon registry and native manuscripts/design.

Material unresolved limits: organic Adobe growth and AI serving economics are not separately disclosed; exact PayPal discretionary excess liquidity is not established; Expedia total AR is an imperfect merchant-cycle proxy and H1 capitalized SBC/parent-only CFO are not separately available; Dropbox paid Dash economics are unquantified. These limits do not prevent the supported recommendation but must remain visible in final underwriting.
